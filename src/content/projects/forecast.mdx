## Context

A grocery chain: 14 stores, tens of thousands of active items, and a replenishment decision
every week for each combination of the two. Over 100,000 product and store series reach the
operational forecast, out of a wider universe above half a million.

That decision used to come from the ERP's forecasting module, a black box that produced a
number but never a reason. Replacing it was not the hard part. **Most of a grocery catalogue
barely moves**, and a single model that looks excellent on fast-moving lines quietly destroys
the long tail.

Two constraints shaped everything. **No cloud budget and no GPU**, so it runs on a shared
Windows server. **A team of two**: I own the data platform and productionisation, the team's
data scientist owns model selection and evaluation.

## Architecture

<Diagram spec={diagrams.forecast} />

**Medallion layout on Delta Lake, local filesystem.** Bronze is raw and append-only, so a bad
transform is always recoverable. Silver holds cleaned series with calendar and promotional
features. Gold holds the training frames. The boundaries exist because that is where the
assertions go.

**Eight models, not one.** Series are split into eight taxonomic categories and a LightGBM
model with a **Tweedie objective** is trained per category. Tweedie because retail demand is
non-negative, zero-inflated and right-skewed, which is exactly the distribution it assumes, and
squared error is not.

**A routing policy on top.** Every series is classified by **Syntetos-Boylan** into smooth,
erratic, intermittent, lumpy or new. Smooth and erratic series use their category's model.
Intermittent, lumpy and new series use a **four-week moving average** instead. That is not a
compromise: a boosted tree trained on a mostly-zero series learns to predict near zero, which
is technically accurate and operationally useless. The moving average is simply better there,
and the honest thing is to ship the model that wins.

**A guardrail on the output.** Daily predictions are capped at `2 × max_8w / 7`, where `max_8w`
is the highest weekly sale in the previous eight complete weeks. It costs almost nothing and it
stops a single bad prediction from ordering a pallet of something nobody buys.

The composite of routing plus guardrail is what runs in production. It is a policy, not a
trained model, which means it can be reasoned about and changed without retraining anything.

**Selection is measured, not argued.** Candidates are scored on three held-out folds before
anything is promoted, and MLflow keeps the runs so a promotion can be defended afterwards with
the evidence still attached.

## The hard part

Months in, I audited the pipeline. Nothing was on fire. That was the point: "everything reports
success" is exactly the state where a silent failure hides.

The audit measured **usable horizon**, how many weeks ahead the system could still forecast from
the data it actually had. That number had been shrinking for weeks.

The cause was structural. The daily ingest had stopped running. The weekly forecast *declared* a
dependency on it but did not *enforce* one, so the orchestrator happily materialised a forecast
from whatever was already in the lake. The job ran, succeeded, and produced a forecast from data
that got staler every week.

Every green checkmark was accurate. The system was still wrong.

The immediate fix made the dependency real. The durable fix was the audit itself: horizon health
is now measured independently, never inferred from job status. **A pipeline's own success
reporting cannot be the evidence that it works.** The failure modes that survive longest are
precisely the ones the reporting does not cover.

## Trade-offs

**A policy instead of one clever model.** Routing between a boosted tree and a moving average is
less impressive on paper than a single architecture that handles everything. It also beats it,
and it can be explained to a buyer in one sentence.

**Reliability before sophistication.** Hierarchical reconciliation is not implemented. It is a
real gap and the wrong next investment while the base pipeline's failure modes are not fully
instrumented.

**Local filesystem over cloud storage.** Zero infrastructure cost and no vendor lock-in, against
horizontal scale and an easy disaster-recovery story. Right at this volume, and the first
decision I would revisit.

**Weekly, not daily.** Replenishment operates weekly. Matching the model's cadence to the
decision's cadence also keeps compute inside the window a shared server can give it.

## Results

**19% less forecast error than the pipeline it replaced.** Scored on the same fold under the
same protocol, WMAPE went from 0.489 to 0.394. On the operational universe the current champion
records **WMAPE 0.296, roughly 70% accuracy**.

Seventy percent deserves context, because out of context it reads like a C grade. This is a
grocery catalogue: most series sell in single digits, many sell nothing most weeks, and accuracy
tracks that exactly. Around **69% on smooth demand**, into the fifties on lumpy and intermittent
series. A forecast that scored 95% here would mean the catalogue had been quietly filtered down
to the easy tenth of it, which is the usual way this number gets inflated. The routing policy is
what stops the long tail dragging the whole thing down.

Over 100,000 product and store combinations forecast weekly across 14 stores, feeding
merchandise reordering directly. Over 370 tests, green before deploy.
