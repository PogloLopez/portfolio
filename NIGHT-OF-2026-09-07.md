## 1. Colour: five projects, five accents

The site was one blue, so five distinct systems read as one undifferentiated block. Each case
study now owns a hue that runs through its card, page header, diagram, demo and prose.

The five were **searched for, not picked**. All sit inside the dark-mode lightness band, clear
the chroma floor, separate by ΔE 14.2 at worst under simulated colour-vision deficiency, and
every one clears WCAG AA for normal text on both surfaces. Measured against this site's own
background, not a generic one.

**One reviewer disagreed with this.** They said five hues on the home grid reads as a template's
default rainbow, and suggested keeping the accent inside each case study but making the grid one
colour. You asked for contrast and differentiation, so I kept it — but that is a real second
opinion and it is your call.

## 2. Motion: a neural network that decomposes as you scroll

Starts assembled over the hero artwork, then comes apart as you scroll: nodes drift outward,
edges stretch and fade past a distance threshold, and the field thins to a scatter by the
footer. Scrolling back up reassembles it, because position is a pure function of scroll rather
than an accumulated animation.

It runs on the **home page only**, and is masked out of the left column. That was not the
original design — see the review section below.

## 3. The performance find

The site was rendering at roughly **15 frames per second**, and I nearly optimised the wrong
thing. I assumed the new canvas was the cost, spent an hour making it cheaper, and got 68ms →
61ms. That barely-moved number was the clue.

Measuring properly: a blank page ran at 16.6ms, and `/about` — which has no canvas at all — ran
at 67ms. Hiding the two background auroras took the frame to 16.7ms.

The cause was `filter: blur(100px)` on two ~780px elements that animate continuously, so both
were re-rasterised every frame. The blur was also **pointless**: it sat on top of a radial
gradient, which is soft by construction. Deleting it and moving the falloff into the gradient's
own colour stops is visually indistinguishable and put the whole site back at **60fps**.

The same pattern was on the landing page and is fixed there too.

## 4. What the adversarial review found

I ran a subagent over both live sites as a hiring manager and as an enterprise buyer. It was
harsh and it was right. The findings worth knowing about:

**The decoration was brighter than the text.** Measured: peak background luminance inside the
`/about` paragraphs was 0.61; the body text is 0.55. Particles were literally brighter than the
sentences they sat behind. Fixed by scoping the field to the home page and masking it away from
the text column — a hard guarantee rather than a matter of opacity.

**Nothing looked clickable.** Tailwind v4 removed `cursor: pointer` from its button preflight,
so every control on the site rendered with the default arrow. The reviewer scrolled past the
model tournament — the best thing on the page — because hovering it did nothing.

**The forecast demo contradicted itself.** It showed the champion tying LightGBM while the copy
underneath claimed it won. That tie is real: on smooth demand the policy routes to that very
model. It now opens on intermittent demand, states the tie, names the branch it routed to, and
gives the gap to the branch it avoided — 51 points, which is the actual argument for routing.

**Two numbers on one screen disagreed.** "70–82% accuracy against roughly 30% from the ERP"
reads as a 2.7× improvement, 200px from a 19% improvement. You had already called the ERP
comparison meaningless; it is gone, and the same-fold reduction leads.

Also fixed: buttons labelled "Generate" that revealed fixed text, a `REPO` field coloured
exactly like a link that did nothing, the home page calling five simulations "working demos",
a disclaimer that argued in a circle, both chat demos opening on three zeros and an empty box,
and a bias of 0.0% claiming the forecast ran high.

## 5. The landing

Repositioned as you asked: the community is no longer the subject. The page names a distance —
what a business needs against what technology already permits — shows three tensions and a
four-step method, and closes on the question rather than an offer.

The reviewer, reading as a buyer, **would not have emailed**. Not because the writing was bad
(they called it the best writing on either site) but because there was no name, no client, no
artefact and no number — a gmail address behind a button they could not forward to a colleague.
Mystery works when there is reason to believe something is behind it.

So there is now a **"Lo construido"** section: three anonymised outcomes with their figure, no
client names, and a link to the portfolio for the detail. The close carries your name and a
visible address.

Also from the review: the CTA gradient failed contrast at 3.9:1 (fixed, both stops now carry
white at AA), the scroll cue was an unlabelled link a screen reader announced as "hashtag
tension", and there was no `og:image` — on a page whose stated channel is referral, every pasted
link was showing without a card. There is one now.
